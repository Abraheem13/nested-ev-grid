Some conclusions are firmer than others; we discuss what learning
contributes, where the voltage correction applies and what limits the findings.

At nominal load, learning does not beat a well-designed rule on cost: the
price-aware heuristic is cheaper than the nested controller,% claim: pa_cheaper_than_nested_all_main
\ and neither the learned prices nor the learned dispatch reduce cost relative
to the plan (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}).% claim: decomp_nested_planprice_costlier_than_plan_S3
% claim: decomp_nested_flatprice_costlier_than_plan_S3
\ The learned levels reduce curtailment, which mattered under stress
(S7);% claim: decomp_nested_less_curt_than_plan_S7
\ the lower unmet energy under stress comes from least-laxity-first
allocation, not from learning.% claim: plan_less_unmet_than_pa_S7
% claim: decomp_nested_more_unmet_than_plan_S7
\ Learned dispatch needs the plan as a starting point: learned from scratch,
it is costlier at both penetration levels tested (S3 and
S5).% claim: abl_abl_no_prior_costlier_S3
% claim: abl_abl_no_prior_costlier_S5
\ The nested design is therefore not a way to beat optimization on cost but a
way to keep learned components where they help while planning and physics
handle the rest. We state this plainly because it is the finding most likely
to be misread: the value of the controller lies in keeping the floor at every
penetration tested, in its small spread over training seeds and in its
behavior under stress, not in a lower energy bill than the heuristic.

Level~3 is corrective control on the simulated quasi-static state, not an a
priori guarantee: it restores the floor whenever the chargers' reactive
headroom suffices and otherwise sheds EV power. Three points qualify it.
First, the inverter rating sets the voltage margin
(Section~\ref{sec:res-abl}, S7). Second, we assume, as in~\cite{leemput2015},
that a charger supplies reactive power at any active power, including zero.
Third, the floor is applied at the medium-voltage (\SI{12.66}{kV}) buses;
service-transformer and secondary drops, switching transients and sub-cycle
dynamics are outside the scope, as in any quasi-static study.

The remaining assumptions bound the generality of the results.
\emph{Information:} the plan, the learned agents and the heuristic know each
plugged-in vehicle's departure and need, as in managed-charging programs where
drivers enter them~\cite{lee2019acn}, but not future arrivals.
\emph{Network:} the feeders are balanced, radial and single-phase equivalent;
unbalance can change the sensitivities the correction uses.
\emph{Behavior:} the acceptance model~\eqref{eq:fishbein} is stylized; the
voltage layer does not depend on it, the value of price signals does.
\emph{Data:} the residential fleet is synthetic; loads (United States) and
prices (Netherlands) come from different systems; training and test days share
the base-load profile; market change is tested only through 2019.
