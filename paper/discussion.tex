\subsection{What the learned levels add}
\label{sec:disc-learning}
The results do not support the claim that learning lowers the energy bill
relative to a well-designed rule: at nominal load the price-aware heuristic,
which executes the Level-2 plan per vehicle, is cheaper than the nested
controller,% claim: pa_cheaper_than_nested_all_main
\ and Table~\ref{tab:decomp} shows that neither the learned prices nor the
learned dispatch reduce cost relative to the plan at S3.% claim: decomp_nested_planprice_costlier_than_plan_S3
% claim: decomp_nested_flatprice_costlier_than_plan_S3
\ What the learned dispatch does provide is a margin: it spreads charging away
from the very cheapest slots, which keeps voltages further from the floor and
leaves Level~3 more reactive headroom. This is irrelevant on most days and
decisive on the worst ones, as S7 shows. The planning prior is essential to
this outcome: learning the same dispatch from scratch is costlier at every
penetration level we tested (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).% claim: abl_abl_no_prior_costlier_S3
% claim: abl_abl_no_prior_costlier_S5
\ We therefore see the nested design less as a way to beat optimization on cost
than as a way to keep learned components where they help (anticipating grid
stress and shaping prices) while planning and physics handle what they handle
well.

\subsection{Scope of the voltage guarantee}
Level~3b is corrective control on the simulated quasi-static state, not an a
priori guarantee. It restores the floor whenever the reactive headroom of the
plugged-in chargers suffices, and otherwise sheds EV power until the floor is
met or the fallback has run its course (Section~\ref{sec:method}). Two consequences follow. First, the
inverter apparent-power rating is the design variable that buys voltage
margin: Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} reports the effect of $S_i$, and S7 shows
the behaviour when the rating is halved. Second, the correction acts at the
\SI{60}{s} resolution of the simulation; switching transients and sub-cycle
dynamics are outside the claim, as for every quasi-static study.

\subsection{Assumptions and limitations}
\emph{Information.} The plan, the learned agents and the heuristic know each
plugged-in vehicle's departure time and remaining need, as in managed-charging
programs where drivers enter them~\cite{lee2019acn}. They do not know future
arrivals. Only the LP-OPF bound sees the whole day in advance.
\emph{Network.} The feeders are balanced, radial and single-phase equivalent;
unbalanced three-phase operation can change the voltage sensitivities on which
the correction step relies.
\emph{Behaviour.} The acceptance model~\eqref{eq:fishbein} is stylized. The
voltage layer does not depend on it, but the value of price signals does.
\emph{Fleets.} The residential fleet is synthetic with stated parameters; the
ACN experiments use recorded sessions from workplace sites.
\emph{Data.} Household loads (Pecan Street, United States) and day-ahead prices
(Netherlands) come from different systems; they provide realistic daily shapes,
not a model of one particular utility. Adaptation to structural market change
is not tested beyond the 2019 price regime.
